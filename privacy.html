Privacy Policy

Nobel Prize Winners for iPhone and iPad
Effective date: 6 November 2026
Developer: Arthur Yessayan

In short
  • Nobel Prize Winners does not collect any personal data. The developer receives nothing from the app.
  • There is no account, no advertising, no analytics, no tracking and no crash-reporting service.
  • Your favorites, recent searches and settings are stored only on your device.
  • The app downloads public information about Nobel laureates and prizes from NobelPrize.org and Wikipedia.
  • Notifications are optional, off by default, and scheduled on your device.

1. Who this policy applies to

This policy covers the Nobel Prize Winners app for iPhone and iPad (the "app"), published by Arthur Yessayan ("we", "us"). It explains what the app stores on your device and what it requests from the internet.

2. What the app stores on your device

  • Favorites: the laureates and prizes you star.
  • Recent searches: up to eight of your latest searches.
  • Settings: your theme and the notification types you turn on.
  • Downloaded content: the Nobel Prize data, laureate photos, and up to 300 Wikipedia biographies you have opened, so the app works offline.

This information stays in the app's private storage on your device and is never sent to us. It is part of your device backups if you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer, under the terms of the backup service you use.

3. What the app requests from the internet

The app has no server of its own. To show its content, it makes requests to these public services:

  • api.nobelprize.org (Nobel Prize Outreach): the list of laureates and prizes. The app checks for new data at most once an hour while it is open, and downloads it again at most once a day.
  • en.wikipedia.org (Wikimedia Foundation): laureate biographies and the addresses of their photos.
  • upload.wikimedia.org (Wikimedia Foundation): the photos themselves.

These requests contain no personal information from the app. They name the app and its version (for example "NobelPrizeWinners/2.0.0"), as Wikimedia asks of all apps, but nothing about you. As with any web request, the services receive your device's IP address and the details of the request. Their handling of this is described in the Wikimedia Foundation privacy policy and on NobelPrize.org.

When you tap a link such as "Read on Wikipedia", "Facts on NobelPrize.org", this policy or Apple's Terms of Use, the page opens in an in-app browser. The website you visit then applies its own privacy policy.

4. Notifications

Notifications are off until you turn one of these on in Settings: Favorites' Birthdays, Milestone Birthdays, Prize Centenaries and Nobel Day. The app then asks for permission. It works out the dates from your favorites and the data already on your device, and schedules local notifications on the device. There are no push notifications and no server that could send them.

5. Sharing and feedback

  • Share opens the iOS share sheet with a short text about a laureate or prize. It goes only where you choose to send it.
  • Send Feedback and Report a Bug open a draft email in your mail app, with the app version and iOS version filled in. Nothing is sent unless you send it.
  • Rate on the App Store opens the App Store.

If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to answer you, and delete it when it is no longer needed.

6. What we do not do

  • We do not collect, receive or store any of your data.
  • No account or sign-in.
  • No advertising, and no tracking across apps or websites. The app does not use the advertising identifier.
  • No analytics and no crash-reporting service.
  • We do not sell or share data, because we have none.

Apple handles your download from the App Store. Apple may give us aggregated, anonymous App Store statistics and, if you chose to share them with developers in your device settings, crash reports. These come from Apple under Apple's privacy policy, not from the app.

7. Deleting your data

  • Favorites: open the Favorites tab, tap Edit, select items and tap Delete, or tap a star again.
  • Recent searches: remove them one by one, or tap Clear in Search.
  • Photos and biographies: Settings → Data & Storage → Clear Cache.
  • Everything: deleting the app removes all of its data from your device.

You can turn notifications off in the app's Settings, or in the iOS Settings app → Notifications → Nobel Prize Winners.

8. Children's privacy

The app does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. It has no account, no chat, and no way to share content with other users.

9. Changes to this policy

If the app's handling of data changes, we will update this page and its effective date before the change is released.
